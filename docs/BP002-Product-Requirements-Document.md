## 1. Purpose

This document defines the product requirements for Project Waypoint.

It translates the philosophy and principles established within **BP001 – The Waypoint Charter** into a practical description of the product, its users, its objectives and the functionality required to achieve those objectives.

This document deliberately avoids implementation details.

Technical architecture, database design and software implementation are documented separately.

---

## 2. Product Summary

Project Waypoint is a digital companion for walkers that enriches every journey by connecting people with trails, local businesses and trusted community knowledge.

Rather than replacing existing navigation software, Waypoint complements it by preserving the experiences that make walking memorable.

The platform enables walkers to discover places they may otherwise overlook, support independent businesses, contribute useful trail knowledge and build a lasting record of their adventures.

Waypoint is designed to become a trusted companion rather than simply another application.

---

## 3. Problem Statement

Current walking applications focus primarily on navigation, route planning and activity tracking.

Whilst these are important, they represent only part of the walking experience.

Walkers often pass interesting cafés, pubs, farm shops, historical sites and other local attractions without ever knowing they exist.

Likewise, independent businesses located close to popular walking routes often struggle to reach walkers despite being ideally placed to serve them.

Practical trail knowledge is frequently shared between walkers but rarely preserved beyond individual conversations.

As a result:

- opportunities are missed
- communities remain disconnected
- local businesses receive little recognition
- memorable experiences fade over time

Waypoint exists to connect these separate parts into a single walking experience.

## 4. Product Objectives

Waypoint has six primary objectives.

### 4.1 Enrich Walking

Encourage walkers to discover more than simply the route itself.

Waypoint should promote curiosity, exploration and memorable experiences.

### 4.2 Support Local Communities

Help independent businesses become recognised as valuable parts of the walking experience.

The relationship should feel natural rather than commercial.

### 4.3 Preserve Adventures

Provide walkers with a lasting record of the places they have explored and the experiences they have collected.

Achievements should celebrate exploration rather than competition.

### 4.4 Build Community Knowledge

Allow walkers to share practical information that helps future visitors.

Community information should be trustworthy, relevant and easy to contribute.

### 4.5 Remain Unobtrusive

The application should enhance walking without becoming the focus of attention.

Interactions should be brief, intuitive and purposeful.

### 4.6 Grow Sustainably

The platform should create long-term value for walkers, businesses and communities without compromising trust.

## 5. User Groups

### Walkers

Individuals exploring trails for recreation, fitness or adventure.

Primary needs include:

- discovering trails
- finding useful facilities
- preserving memories
- collecting achievements
- supporting local businesses
- contributing useful information

### Businesses

Independent organisations located near walking routes.

Examples include:

- cafés
- pubs
- farm shops
- campsites
- outdoor retailers
- visitor attractions

Primary needs include:

- attracting walkers
- increasing repeat visits
- promoting offers
- understanding customer engagement
- becoming recognised within the walking community

### Administrators

Responsible for maintaining the platform.

Primary responsibilities include:

- approving businesses
- moderating community content
- managing trails
- resolving reports
- maintaining data quality

---

## 6. Core Product Loop

1. A walker discovers a trail.
2. They complete the walk.
3. They discover places along the route.
4. They visit participating businesses.
5. They collect passport stamps.
6. They contribute useful trail information.
7. Their personal journey grows.
8. Businesses benefit from increased footfall.
9. Future walkers benefit from community knowledge.
10. The cycle repeats.

Every major feature should strengthen this loop.

## 8. MVP Scope

Included:

- User accounts
- Trail directory
- Trail details
- Business directory
- Business profiles
- QR check-ins
- Passport stamps
- Basic achievements
- Community reports
- Business dashboard
- Administration portal

Excluded:

- Route planning
- Turn-by-turn navigation
- Clubs
- Events
- Messaging
- Accommodation booking
- Advanced analytics
- AI recommendations
- Wearables
- International trails

---

## 9. Product Principles

Every feature should satisfy at least one of the following.

- Makes walking more enjoyable.
- Supports local businesses.
- Improves community knowledge.
- Preserves memories.
- Improves trust.
- Simplifies administration.

If a feature satisfies none of these principles, it should not be included.

---

## 10. Success Measures

The success of Waypoint should be measured through meaningful engagement rather than installation numbers.

Examples include:

Walkers:

- walks completed
- passport stamps collected
- return users
- community contributions

Businesses:

- participating businesses
- repeat visitors
- offer redemptions
- business retention

Platform:

- verified reports
- active users
- trail coverage
- community trust

---

## 11. Risks

The primary product risks are:

- insufficient business adoption
- insufficient walker adoption
- poor quality community information
- feature creep
- overcomplicating the user experience
- loss of user trust

Every future decision should seek to reduce one or more of these risks.

---

## 12. Definition of Success

Waypoint succeeds when:

Walkers begin opening the application before every walk because it has become part of their routine.

Businesses recognise Waypoint as a genuine source of new visitors.

Communities see the platform as a trustworthy source of local knowledge.

The application quietly enriches the walking experience without becoming its focus.
