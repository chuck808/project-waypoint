# Field Guide 005 – Stamps

The stamp is Waypoint's identity asset. The waypoint marker is for
wayfinding; the stamp is for remembering. They are different jobs.

A Waypoint stamp is a venue's own mark, pressed onto the walker's
passport. Five rules govern every stamp:

## 1. Ink, not paint

Stamps are stroke-only line work on the paper background. No fills, no
gradients, no backgrounds. A rubber stamp is ink on paper.

## 2. Each venue is a mark, not an icon in a circle

Frame shape, a small motif, and arced venue text combine into a mark
that belongs to one place. The Ramblers Rest is a round cachet with the
Great Ridge; the Old Barn is a rounded square with its barn. Unknown
venues degrade to a monogram in their category's ink — distinctive by
default, never generic.

## 3. Ink colour is the category

See Field Guide 002. Ink is the only place category colour appears.

## 4. The jitter is the humanity

Every stamp sits at a slight tilt, derived deterministically from the
moment it records. Hand-pressed, not machine-placed; stable, never
fidgeting.

## 5. The stamp is the success moment

When a visit is remembered, the venue's mark lands on the page with the
date pressed into its rim. The moment of delight is the identity
artifact. No generic ticks.

## Future

"Design your stamp" belongs to the Business Portal: a constrained kit of
frames, motifs and inks, so every mark is distinctive and none can be
ugly. Until then, bespoke marks are hand-made and few — which is how
good stamps have always been.
