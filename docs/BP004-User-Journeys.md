## 1. Purpose

This document describes how different users experience Project Waypoint.

Rather than describing screens or workflows, it describes complete journeys from the user's perspective.

Every feature implemented within Waypoint should support one or more of these journeys.

---

## 2. Journey One — Planning a Day's Walk

**Persona:** Explorer

Emma has a free Saturday and wants a scenic walk with somewhere pleasant to stop afterwards.

She opens Waypoint. The home screen offers inspiration rather than complexity. She browses nearby regions and discovers a circular walk she has never completed.

The trail page provides photographs, estimated duration, terrain, difficulty, facilities, nearby businesses, points of interest, and community updates.

She notices a café popular with walkers, an honesty box selling cakes, a nearby viewpoint, and a recent report mentioning muddy ground.

She saves the walk, downloads offline information, and leaves home.

**Product Outcome:** Waypoint adds value before the walk begins.

---

## 3. Journey Two — Walking the Trail

**Persona:** Explorer

Emma begins the walk.

Waypoint quietly records progress. The phone remains in her pocket for most of the walk.

At appropriate moments she checks her location, nearby facilities, and recent community reports.

The application never interrupts unnecessarily.

At a viewpoint, Waypoint offers the opportunity to collect a location stamp without demanding attention.

**Product Outcome:** Technology supports the experience rather than becoming it.

---

## 4. Journey Three — Discovering Local Businesses

**Persona:** Explorer / Business Owner

Emma finishes the walk and sees that a participating café is five minutes away.

She visits. The café displays a Waypoint Partner sticker. After ordering coffee, she scans a QR code beside the till.

Waypoint awards a café passport stamp, trail completion stamp, and local collection progress.

The café owner later sees another Waypoint visitor recorded in the business dashboard.

**Product Outcome:** Everyone benefits naturally.

---

## 5. Journey Four — Contributing Back

**Persona:** Community Contributor

During another walk Emma notices a damaged footbridge.

Waypoint lets her submit a report within seconds. She chooses Broken Bridge, adds a photograph, writes a short description, and submits.

Other walkers later confirm the report. Once repaired, someone marks it resolved.

**Product Outcome:** Community knowledge grows while remaining trustworthy.

---

## 6. Journey Five — Looking Back

**Persona:** Collector

That evening Emma reviews the day's adventure.

Waypoint records the trail completed, distance walked, time taken, businesses visited, passport stamps collected, collection progress, and community contribution.

Rather than presenting statistics alone, Waypoint presents the day as another page in her walking history.

**Product Outcome:** The journey continues after the walk ends.

---

## 7. Journey Six — Joining as a Business

**Persona:** Business Owner

David owns a small village café.

He registers through the Waypoint business portal, provides business details, opening hours, photographs, facilities, and an optional offer.

Once approved, he receives a business profile, partner badge, printable QR code, and dashboard access.

He places the QR code beside the till. Nothing else changes.

**Product Outcome:** Participation is simple enough for non-technical business owners.

---

## 8. Journey Seven — Managing the Platform

**Persona:** Administrator

A new community report is submitted.

The moderation dashboard highlights it. The administrator reviews location, photograph, description, and community confirmations.

Genuine reports become visible. Spam is removed. Contributors receive appropriate feedback.

**Product Outcome:** Trust remains one of the platform's greatest strengths.

## 9. The Complete Product Loop

```text
Choose a trail
        ↓
Complete the walk
        ↓
Discover places
        ↓
Visit local businesses
        ↓
Collect experiences
        ↓
Share useful knowledge
        ↓
Help future walkers
        ↓
Strengthen communities
        ↓
Begin another journey
```

---

## 10. Design Implications

Waypoint should:

- minimise interaction whilst walking
- maximise discovery before and after walking
- make business participation effortless
- encourage meaningful community contributions
- preserve memories without becoming a social network
- reward exploration rather than competition
