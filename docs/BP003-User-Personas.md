## 1. Purpose

This document defines the primary user personas for Project Waypoint.

These personas represent behavioural archetypes rather than demographic profiles.

A single user may identify with several personas depending upon the type of walk they are undertaking.

---

## 2. Primary Personas

### Persona 001 — The Explorer

The Explorer enjoys discovering new places.

**Goals**

- Discover new trails.
- Find interesting places.
- Experience local culture.
- Build lasting memories.
- Explore areas they have never visited.

**Needs**

- Reliable trail information.
- Interesting recommendations.
- Authentic local businesses.
- Useful facilities.
- Easy navigation around the day's activities.

**Frustrations**

- Tourist traps.
- Generic recommendations.
- Missing interesting places.
- Poor quality information.
- Overly complicated applications.

**Measure of Success**

> "I've discovered somewhere I never knew existed."

**Design Implications**

Waypoint should encourage discovery, recommend authentic locations, celebrate exploration, and minimise unnecessary interaction.

---

### Persona 002 — The Collector

The Collector enjoys building a personal history of their adventures.

Achievements matter because they represent experiences rather than competition.

**Goals**

- Complete trails.
- Collect passport stamps.
- Build collections.
- Record achievements.
- Preserve memories.

**Needs**

- Meaningful achievements.
- Clear progress.
- Attractive collections.
- Personal history.
- Long-term statistics.

**Frustrations**

- Pointless badges.
- Artificial gamification.
- Lost progress.
- Temporary achievements.

**Measure of Success**

> "My passport tells the story of my adventures."

**Design Implications**

Waypoint should reward meaningful experiences, preserve history permanently, and avoid excessive gamification.

---

### Persona 003 — The Community Contributor

The Contributor enjoys helping other walkers.

**Goals**

- Improve trail information.
- Help other walkers.
- Report changes.
- Recommend worthwhile places.

**Needs**

- Simple reporting.
- Community recognition.
- Trustworthy moderation.
- Feedback that contributions matter.

**Frustrations**

- Spam.
- False information.
- Ignored reports.
- Complicated reporting processes.

**Measure of Success**

> "My contribution genuinely helped someone."

**Design Implications**

Waypoint should make reporting effortless, verify community knowledge, and reward quality rather than quantity.

---

### Persona 004 — The Local Business Owner

The Business Owner wants to welcome walkers without becoming a marketer.

**Goals**

- Increase footfall.
- Build repeat customers.
- Become recognised locally.
- Promote their business honestly.

**Needs**

- Simple onboarding.
- Easy offer management.
- Visitor analytics.
- Affordable participation.

**Frustrations**

- Expensive advertising.
- Complex software.
- Poor customer engagement.
- Lack of measurable results.

**Measure of Success**

> "I can clearly see that Waypoint brings walkers through my door."

**Design Implications**

Waypoint should remain simple, demonstrate measurable value, and require minimal maintenance.

---

### Persona 005 — The Weekend Family

Walking is a shared activity.

**Goals**

- Enjoy a day outdoors.
- Discover family-friendly places.
- Keep children engaged.
- Create memories together.

**Needs**

- Easy trails.
- Facilities.
- Food and drink.
- Interesting stopping points.
- Family achievements.

**Frustrations**

- Boring routes.
- Poor facilities.
- Difficult navigation.
- Nothing for children.

**Measure of Success**

> "We all had a brilliant day."

---

### Persona 006 — The Long-Distance Walker

The Long-Distance Walker enjoys extended journeys.

**Goals**

- Complete long routes.
- Track progress.
- Locate facilities.
- Preserve the journey.

**Needs**

- Reliable information.
- Offline capability.
- Practical facilities.
- Journey history.

**Frustrations**

- Incorrect information.
- Missing facilities.
- Poor offline support.
- Losing progress.

**Measure of Success**

> "I completed the journey knowing Waypoint was there when I needed it."

---

## 5. Design Principles Derived from Personas

The application should:

- reward exploration rather than competition
- support local communities
- remain simple to use
- preserve memories
- encourage trusted community contributions
- minimise user effort
- remain valuable over many years
